Delta and gamma: why hedging changes as price moves

A 20,000 call. Delta is how many NQ contracts one option acts like. Gamma is how fast delta changes as price moves. Drag the price across the strike, then shrink the time left and watch gamma turn into a spike.

Time to expiry

The takeaway: gamma is biggest at the strike and near expiry. That is where dealers have to trade the most futures for each point price moves, so that is where their hedging pushes price the hardest.

Same news, two regimes

Both charts get the exact same random news shocks (the faint dashed line is price with no dealers). On the left, dealers are long gamma, so they sell rallies and buy dips. On the right, they are short gamma, so they chase. Nothing else differs.

Amplifying

Long gamma: price rises, the dealer's calls gain delta, so the dealer sells futures into the rally and buys them back on the dip. That pushes against every move and pins price near the big strike. Short gamma is the mirror image: the dealer sells into drops and buys into rips, which stretches every move.
