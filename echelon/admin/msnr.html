THE MSNR × QM PLAYBOOK

Malaysian Support and Resistance: close-based levels, the fresh filter, the Quasimodo entry, the sibling setups, and an honest read on whether it works.

Prepared for: D1

Research date: August 22, 2026. Sources: Malaysian SnR / Alchemist educational material, RTM (ReadTheMarket) references, TradingView indicator implementations, Forex Factory practitioner threads. Key links in the Appendix.

Read this first

MSNR is a discretionary price-action school, not a quantified system. No rigorous large-sample backtest of the QM with objective rules exists; the advertised win rates (72–82%) are marketing. Practitioners who actually trade it report 30–50% hit rates carried by 2R+ payoffs. The mechanism — stop run, structure shift, one retest — is the same engine as LIT. Treat this as a vocabulary and a filter set, not a new system.

0. The method in one page

  1. Mark levels from closes, never wicks. Switch to a line chart (or read candle closes). Peaks are A-levels, valleys are V-levels. The wick beyond a close is not the level — it is the liquidity that fuels the trap later.
  2. Only trade fresh. A level is fresh until the first wick touches it. One touch spends it. A body closing through it flips it to the other side and resets it fresh.
  3. A MISS validates a level. Wicks that try to reach back to a level and fail are evidence the market is respecting it.
  4. The QM is the flagship reversal entry: price sweeps beyond a fresh higher-timeframe level (the head), breaks the prior swing (structure shift), then retests the left-shoulder close (the QML) exactly once. Limit at the QML.
  5. Stop beyond the head, never the right shoulder. A violation of the head kills the trap premise. Target the next fresh opposing level; minimum 2R or skip.
  6. The QM in the middle of nowhere is noise. The confluence IS the fresh HTF level behind the head. No level, no trade.
  7. Continuation twin: the SBR/RBS flip (or RTM's FTR/FTB) — first retest of a freshly flipped level in the new direction.
  8. Expect 35–50% wins at 2R+. The edge, if you have one, comes from the payoff and the filter, not the hit rate. Judge over 20–30 journaled setups.

1. What MSNR is

MSNR = Malaysian Support and Resistance (Malaysian SnR), a method built by Malaysian traders and popularized by the “Alchemist” school. Its premise: the market respects precise close-based horizontal levels, not zones and not wick extremes. Levels are marked off the line chart, qualified by whether they are still fresh, and traded on their first return. The Quasimodo is taught inside this framework as the primary reversal entry — the pattern itself is inherited from RTM (ReadTheMarket), the school that also produced FTR, flag limits and compression (section 9).

2. The levels: A, V, gap

  • A-level — a peak on the line chart (close-based swing high, shaped like the letter A). Resistance.
  • V-level — a valley on the line chart (close-based swing low). Support.
  • Gap level — the hidden one: two consecutive same-color candles whose bodies leave a close-to-open gap. Almost nobody marks these, so they are usually still fresh when price returns.

3. Fresh, unfresh, reset — and the MISS

Every level is born fresh. The first wick that touches it spends it. A body closing through it flips it to the other side and it is fresh again. A MISS is a level that later wicks try and fail to reach — each miss upgrades the level.

4. Storyline and roadblocks

The trend overlay. A storyline begins when price taps a fresh higher-timeframe level and rejects; that storyline only ends at another level on the same timeframe. Everything in between is delivery. Roadblocks are the fresh levels one timeframe lower where pullbacks are likely mid-storyline — they are where you add or re-enter in the storyline direction, and where you expect targets to stall. This is how MSNR traders ride a move instead of only picking turns.

5. The QM entry (sell side)

Five swings: higher high above the prior high (trap), collapse below the prior low (shift), one retrace to the left-shoulder level (entry). In LIT language: the head is the inducement, the break is the shift, the QML retest is the mitigation. The confluence rule that makes it work: the head must sweep a fresh HTF level. The QML itself does not need to be fresh — the fresh filter applies to the level behind the head.

  1. Uptrend delivers into the level. The move only matters because price is arriving at a fresh higher-timeframe A-level.
  2. Left shoulder forms. A swing high prints and pulls back. Its close is the future QML — mark it now.
  3. Prior low. The pullback low between shoulder and head. The tripwire.
  4. Head sweeps the fresh level. Wick through, close back beneath. Breakout buyers trapped, stops above the shoulder taken.
  5. Break of the prior low. A body closes below it. The QM is now valid — until this close, you have nothing.
  6. Retest of the QML → entry. One retrace into the left-shoulder level. Limit at the QML, or wait for the rejection candle.

6. Execution: entry, stop, target

Skip it when: the HTF level is unfresh; the QM forms mid-range with no level behind the head; price never sweeps beyond the shoulder; the next fresh opposing level is closer than 2R. No fresh level, no trade.

8. The other MSNR entries

  • SBR/RBS flip. The continuation counterpart of the QM. A fresh level breaks with a body close, flips sides, resets fresh — entry on the first retest of the flipped level in the new direction. Where QM catches the turn, the flip catches the continuation after the break.
  • Gap-level entry. Price returns to fill a body gap between two same-color candles and reacts. Traded like a fresh A/V retest; because almost nobody marks gaps, they are usually still fresh.
  • Fresh-level bounce with MISS validation. No pattern at all: fade the first touch of a fresh level that has one or more misses behind it. Highest frequency, lowest context — the QM is essentially this entry plus a sweep for confirmation.
  • Roadblock pullback. Inside an active storyline, enter at the fresh roadblock one timeframe lower, in the storyline direction, until the opposing HTF level is reached.

9. The RTM family (where QM comes from)

  • FTR / FTB. The closest sibling. Price breaks a strong level, retraces but fails to return all the way, bases early and continues — that base is the FTR. The FTB (first time back) is the entry: first return to the FTR zone, stop beyond it, target the next level. Same one-clean-retest logic as QM, but with the trend.
  • Flag Limit (FL). The consolidation base after an impulsive pole (rally-base-rally / drop-base-drop). Price travels from one flag limit to the next; an engulf of one FL targets the next. FLs are the map, QM/FTB are the entries on it.
  • Compression (CP). A quality filter: momentum grinding weaker into a zone, eating liquidity on approach. Compression into a fresh level is fuel for the reversal — a QM whose retrace compresses into the QML is the A+ version.
  • Caps. False-breakout structures that cap price, absorb breakout traders, and reverse. Conceptually the QM's head as a standalone pattern.
  • MPL (Maximum Pain Level). Where the largest pool of trapped traders sits — RTM's framing of where sweeps aim. Deeper cuts in the catalog: engulf, Can Can, diamond, 3-drives.

10. Does it work? The honest read

  • No rigorous independent evidence exists. QM cannot be cleanly codified, so every backtest bakes in the tester's discretion. TradingFinder ships a QM backtest strategy on TradingView you can run on NQ yourself (set margin_long/margin_short or futures entries silently decline).
  • Advertised win rates are inflated. 72% (crypto article), 82% (YouTube scalping thumbnail) — small samples, cherry-picked, no costs.
  • Practitioners report 30–50% hit rates at 2R+, on M30 and above; QM indicators flag dozens of shapes and most are junk — the fresh-HTF-level filter is the whole game. Stop placement is the hard part.
  • The defensible version: fresh HTF level + actual sweep + body-close break + asymmetric R:R. The naked shape mid-range is what produces the junk signals.
  • Verdict procedure: forward-test the filtered version for 20–30 journaled setups in TradeDesk. Expect ~35–50% wins at 2R+ if it is earning its place.

11. Mapping to LIT

The QM is BuildUp → Inducement → Mitigation with a Malaysian accent: the head is the inducement (stop run beyond a level), the break is the shift, the QML retest is the mitigation entry. The two genuinely new ideas worth absorbing:

  1. The fresh/unfresh discipline — mark from closes and refuse any level a wick already spent. A stricter filter than most mitigation entries use.
  2. The body-close reset rule — an objective, mechanical answer for when a broken level becomes tradeable from the other side.

Both slot into trap qualification without touching the LIT vocabulary. FTB is a mitigation entry; the SBR/RBS flip is a BuildUp resolving; compression is trap fuel.
